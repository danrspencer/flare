How flares work

Part of Architecture, for contributors and the curious. Nothing here is needed to use FLARE.

A flare (light.<name>_flare) is a light that fronts a room automation, so a voice assistant, HomeKit or a dashboard can switch a room without going around it. Saying “turn the kitchen on” to Siri goes to the flare, and the flare runs the kitchen’s automation. Siri’s own “on” sent straight to the bulbs would read as an override, and the room would be left at whatever the bulbs restored to.

What it is in code

FlareLight (light.py) subclasses HA’s own LightGroup, so state, brightness and colour are the group’s, worked out from its members. Two things differ from a plain light group: who its members are, and what “on” means.

Membership

A flare stores two things: its automation, by entity-registry id so a rename doesn’t orphan it, and the name of the input holding the room’s lights (room_target for our blueprint). It reads the input’s value live. A plain automation, not made from a blueprint, stores a target instead.

  • The inputs come from the automation entity’s private _blueprint_inputs (flares/automation.py:blueprint_inputs). Its raw_config is the substituted config, so it doesn’t have them. This is an accepted risk, kept to that one function, and the behaviour tests break if HA changes it.
  • Members are tracked with async_track_target_selector_state_change_event, HA’s own target resolution: the same rule as flare.resolve_target, so a flare’s members are exactly its room’s lights.
  • It re-resolves on automation_reloaded, on entity-registry changes to automations, and once at homeassistant_started (automations load after integrations).
  • A flare is never one of a room’s lights. The room’s tick would send it the curve, and the flare would pass that on to every bulb as an override. Three layers stop it: the blueprint rejects integration_entities('flare'), the services drop flare lights (flares/identity.without_flares), and a flare’s own member filter drops other flares. Without the last, a flare could contain itself and recurse forever.

On and off 

flowchart TB
    on["light.turn_on on the flare"] --> bare{"bare?<br/>(no keys but transition)"}
    bare -->|"yes"| trig["automation.trigger<br/>→ manual run: allow_turn_on, force"]
    bare -->|"no, values given"| mark1["flare.claims_override(members, room)"]
    mark1 --> grp1["LightGroup.turn_on(values)"]
    off["light.turn_off on the flare"] --> mark2["flare.claims_override(members, room)"]
    mark2 --> grp2["LightGroup.turn_off<br/>(a plain light.turn_off)"]
  • A bare “on” runs the automation. How a room comes on is the automation’s decision. A manual run passes allow_turn_on and force, so “on” also takes back a room that was overridden.
  • “On” with values, and “off”, mark the lights overridden first, with the automation as the room, then send as any light group does. Without that, an unclaimed light (every light, once a room goes dark) would read untracked, and the run the change itself triggers would take it straight back to the curve. That’s how Siri’s “100 %” got dimmed back down. The override claim has a fresh context and no target, so it never matches; the room going dark hands the lights back.
  • Off is a plain light.turn_off, never flare.turn_off. A flare’s off is the user’s. Recording it as FLARE’s off would let a run landing while the room was half-off switch it back on.

It reaches flare.claims_override as a service call because that’s the Settings entry’s, which may not be loaded. has_service checks first.

The light platform only loads with flares

The Flares entry forwards the light platform only when it has at least one flare, and remembers whether it did in entry.runtime_data so unload matches. An empty entry has nothing to put on it, and setting light up that early interfered with the behaviour tests’ fake bulbs.

Adding flares

  • Add Flare lists only automations made from our blueprint. Any other automation goes through a custom path offering only inputs that can hold lights (lights_input_kinds).
  • Area setup adds them in bulk, one subentry each, and the entry’s update listener coalesces the reloads into one (_async_reload_entry).
  • Nothing creates a flare on its own except area setup, where the user ticks the rooms. A HomeKit bridge including light, or a voice assistant exposing new entities, would otherwise put every room in the Home app after an update.
  • A new flare’s device goes in its automation’s area once, at creation (_place_in_area). After that, the area is the user’s.
