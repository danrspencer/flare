Override protection

FLARE records what it last asked each light for, so it can tell its own changes from anyone else’s and leave alone a light you’ve changed yourself. Its entities and options are under Settings → Devices & Services → FLARE → Settings.

For what override protection means for your lights day to day, see the Override protection guide.

FLARE’s entities

Entity Description
sensor.flare_controlled_lights The number of lights FLARE is setting. For information only.
sensor.flare_overridden_lights The number of lights something else has changed. For information only.
button.flare_clear Discards every claim.
sensor.<light>_flare_status One for each light a room has named: its status, on the light’s own device. rooms lists the rooms it’s in, claimed whether FLARE has a claim on it, asked what FLARE last asked it for, and differs which of that it isn’t showing (state, brightness, color), within the tolerances.

The two counts and each light’s status are recorded in history, with long-term statistics for the counts. asked and differs aren’t recorded, since they change with every update. A light that’s unavailable or has no claim isn’t included in either count. The dashboard shows the counts and each light for each area too.

FLARE keeps its claims in Home Assistant’s own storage, so they’re kept across a restart. To see every claim at once, download the diagnostics from FLARE → Settings: they list each light’s claims and each room’s lights. They also list the last 50 overrides, each with what FLARE asked for, what the light reported and what kind of light it is.

A light’s status sensor appears the first time a room names the light, under Diagnostic on the light’s device page, and stays after its room lets the light go. It’s unavailable while the light is, and it’s removed with the light. A light with no device, such as a light group, gets one named after it instead. An automation can trigger on it:

triggers:
  - trigger: state
    entity_id: sensor.kitchen_1_flare_status
    to: overridden

Clear discards every claim, not only for the overridden lights. FLARE then sets every light again on its next update.

How it works

FLARE tells a light you’ve changed from one it set by keeping a record of what it last asked each light for, and comparing the light with it.

Claims

Each time FLARE sets a light, it records a claim for the light: what it asked the light for. On every update, FLARE compares each light with its claim, and that comparison gives the light a status:

The light Status What FLARE’s updates do
Matches its claim controlled Keep setting it to the curve.
Has stopped matching its claim, for less than 30 seconds mismatched Leave it alone for now.
Hasn’t matched its claim for 30 seconds overridden Leave it alone.
Has no claim untracked Set it, which gives it a claim.

“Matches” means within a tolerance, since bulbs rarely report back exactly what they were sent: 5% of the brightness and 5 mireds of the colour temperature by default. See how close counts as a match.

The 30 seconds give a slow bulb time to report back, so a light that’s only catching up isn’t counted as overridden.

Example: dimming a lamp

The living room has a ceiling light and a lamp, and someone’s in the room.

Time What happens The lamp’s claim The lamp Status
19:00 FLARE sets the room on, 180, 3000 K on, 180, 3000 K controlled
19:20 You dim the lamp in the Home app on, 180, 3000 K on, 60, 3000 K mismatched
19:20:30   on, 180, 3000 K on, 60, 3000 K overridden
19:21 FLARE’s next update on, 180, 3000 K on, 60, 3000 K overridden
23:00 You switch both lights off deleted off off
07:00 Motion in the morning on, 255, 6667 K on, 255, 6667 K controlled

From 19:21 the update keeps setting the ceiling light to the curve, but leaves the lamp out, because it’s overridden. So the lamp stays at 60 all evening.

At 23:00 none of the room’s lights is on, so the room deletes their claims (see how a light goes back to the curve). In the morning the lamp has no claim, so it’s untracked, and the update sets it with the rest of the room.

Example: switching a lamp off

Switching a light off counts as changing it, the same as dimming it. With the same room:

Time What happens The lamp’s claim The lamp Status
19:00 FLARE sets the room on, 180, 3000 K on, 180, 3000 K controlled
20:00 You switch the lamp off on, 180, 3000 K off mismatched
20:00:30   on, 180, 3000 K off overridden
20:01 FLARE’s next update on, 180, 3000 K off overridden

The ceiling light is still on, so the room keeps its claims, and every update leaves the lamp off. Once you switch the ceiling light off too, the room deletes both claims, and both lights come on next time.

Lights FLARE switches off

When FLARE switches a light off itself, it sets the light’s claim to off first. The light then matches its claim, so it’s controlled rather than overridden, and it comes on again with the rest of the room. That happens when:

  • a light’s brightness level is 0, such as a light excluded in the current phase,
  • a room empties and Idle Brightness leaves a nightlight on, and the room’s other lights are switched off.

In that second case the nightlight keeps the room from going dark, so the room keeps its claims. Because the other lights’ claims say off, they come on when someone walks back in.

If your own automation switches lights off, the flare.turn_off or light.turn_off comparison shows which to use.

Back to the curve

An overridden light goes back to the curve once its claim is gone, or once FLARE sets it regardless:

  • The room goes dark. Once none of the room’s claimed lights is on, the room deletes all their claims. A light that’s unavailable counts as off, so one dead bulb can’t hold a room’s claims forever. A light in the room that FLARE has no claim on, such as a lamp another automation runs, doesn’t count either way.
  • You press Clear. Clear under FLARE → Settings, or an area’s Clear on the dashboard, deletes claims, so FLARE sets those lights at its next update.
  • You run the room’s automation yourself, or turn on the room’s flare with no brightness or colour. That sets every light, overridden ones included, and records new claims.
  • The light goes unavailable. Its claim is deleted, so it’s untracked when it comes back.

A restart doesn’t send a light back: claims are kept across restarts, and a light still showing what its claim says is still controlled.

When a room’s lights come back online together, such as when they’re switched on at the wall, FLARE waits a few seconds before setting them. A bulb starts up at whatever it powers up as and misses a command sent while it boots, which would leave it not matching its claim.

Two claims, not one

Each light actually has two claims: the last thing FLARE sent it, and the last thing FLARE saw it show. A light matching either one is controlled. So if a command never reaches a bulb, such as a dropped Zigbee message, the bulb still matches the older claim and isn’t counted as overridden. FLARE sends the command again at its next update.

What the services record

flare.apply_lighting records a claim on each light it sets, and flare.turn_off sets the claim of each light it switches off to off. A light that’s on and has no claim is always sent what flare.apply_lighting would set, even if it already shows it, and that write claims it. After a Clear, for example, a room’s lights are claimed again on their next update. Without a room_id, a light already showing the values isn’t sent anything.

Rooms

Claims belong to the lights, not to any one automation, so two automations setting the same light don’t count each other’s changes as overrides. Each room automation names its lights, and that’s what decides which claims are deleted together when the room goes dark. If a light is in two rooms, its claim is deleted when either room goes dark.

Each call that records claims names a room, usually the automation making it, and the room’s lights. The blueprint passes its own automation as room_id and its Lights & Occupancy lights as room_entities. If you call the services yourself, do the same:

action: flare.apply_lighting
data:
  entities: [light.kitchen_1, light.kitchen_2]
  brightness: 200
  color_temp_kelvin: 3200
  transition: 2
  room_id: "{{ this.entity_id }}"
  room_entities: [light.kitchen_1, light.kitchen_2, light.kitchen_lamp]
  • Without room_id, apply_lighting sets the lights but records nothing, and doesn’t leave any light alone.
  • turn_off doesn’t take a room: it updates the claims of the lights FLARE already has, which already belong to their rooms.
  • claims_record and claims_override require room_id. claims_check and claims_clear don’t take one.
  • Without room_entities, a room’s lights are the ones its calls have set.
  • force: true sets lights even if something else has changed them, and records the claim.
  • A light FLARE has no claim on is free for FLARE to set, so a change your own automation makes to one isn’t left alone unless you mark it with flare.claims_override.

If an automation is renamed, its room carries on: FLARE keeps rooms by the automation’s entity registry entry, not its entity ID.

How close counts as a match

A light matches a claim’s values if it’s within a tolerance of them, so only a change you’d notice counts as someone else’s. Bulbs rarely report back exactly what they were sent: a colour temperature asked for as 4294 K can come back as 4312 K. You can set the tolerances under FLARE → Settings → Configure:

Option Default Description
Brightness tolerance 5% How far, as a percentage of the brightness FLARE asked for, a light’s brightness can be and still match. A dim light always gets at least 2 (of 255).
Colour temperature tolerance 5 mireds How far a light’s colour temperature can be from what FLARE asked for and still match. For a light FLARE sets by RGB, the red, green and blue differences added together can be up to twice this (10 by default).

The same tolerances decide what’s worth sending: a light that close to its target isn’t sent anything.

If lights are marked overridden when nobody has changed them, raise the one that matches the difference shown on the dashboard. Lower them to catch smaller changes.

FLARE’s events

FLARE says when a room takes its lights, when it lets them go, and when something else changes one of them. Each event is filed under one of FLARE’s entities (its Controlled or Overridden count, or Clear for a release) and FLARE’s device, so they appear in its Activity. room names the automation. FLARE adjusting lights it already has isn’t announced.

When a room starts controlling lights it wasn’t, such as when it comes on, FLARE fires flare_lights_controlled once for them all, a few seconds after the first, since the bulbs report back one by one:

lights: [light.kitchen_1, light.kitchen_2]   # the lights it took
controlled: 6                                # how many of its lights FLARE is controlling now
room: automation.kitchen_lighting
entity_id: sensor.flare_controlled_lights
device_id: ...                               # FLARE's device

Controlled lights Kitchen Lighting is now controlling 6 lights

When a room lets its lights go, because it went dark or you pressed Clear, FLARE fires flare_lights_released:

lights: [light.kitchen_1, light.kitchen_2]
room: automation.kitchen_lighting
entity_id: button.flare_clear
device_id: ...

Clear Kitchen Lighting cleared 6 lights

When something else changes one of FLARE’s lights, and it stays changed for 30 seconds, FLARE fires flare_light_overridden for that light:

light: light.kitchen_1
rooms: [automation.kitchen_lighting]   # every room it's one of
entity_id: sensor.flare_overridden_lights
device_id: ...
previous_status: controlled
live_context_id: 01M11...
live: { state: on, brightness: 12, color_temp_kelvin: 6500 }   # or rgb_color, for a colour
observed: { context_id: ..., target: {...}, recorded_at: ... }
latest:   { context_id: ..., target: {...}, recorded_at: ... }

Overridden lights Kitchen Lighting released Kitchen 1 to something else (last asked for 255/6667, found 12/6500)

live is what the light was showing: its brightness and colour temperature, or its rgb_color if it wasn’t showing a white. A light reporting its colour as xy that’s showing a white has its colour temperature worked out from it.

It fires once when the light changes hands, not again while the light stays overridden, and not for lights that were already overridden before a restart. To trigger an automation on it:

triggers:
  - trigger: event
    event_type: flare_light_overridden

Inspecting tracked state

flare.claims_check returns each light’s status, and each light’s status sensor shows it:

Status Meaning
controlled FLARE is setting it: it matches one of its claims.
overridden It has matched neither claim for at least 30 seconds, so something else has changed it.
mismatched It has matched neither claim for less than 30 seconds. Usually it’s FLARE’s own change still arriving, or a light just back online reporting late, so it isn’t counted or announced as overridden yet. It’s left alone meanwhile, the same as an overridden light.
untracked It’s on, and has no claim, or only one FLARE hasn’t seen the light report yet. FLARE sets it as usual.
off It’s off and has no claim. A light FLARE turned off is controlled; one someone else turned off is overridden.
unavailable Home Assistant can’t reach it, so its claims aren’t checked. It isn’t blocked.

matched_via says which claim matched, and how: latest-context, latest-value, observed-context or observed-value. rooms lists the rooms the light is one of.

Each claim has a recorded_at time and a context_id, which you can use to find the change in Home Assistant’s logbook. A light’s claim follows it when it’s renamed, and goes when it’s deleted from Home Assistant.

When rooms update

FLARE fires a flare_tick event for each area once per update interval, carrying the area as area_id. Areas tick one after another, in name order, a gap apart, so rooms don’t all send commands at the same time. A tick is a plain event, not an entity, so it never appears in Activity, history or the logbook. You can set the interval and the gap under FLARE → Settings → Configure:

Option Default Description
Update interval 1 minute How often each area ticks, from 1 to 60 minutes.
Gap between areas 1 second Reduced automatically when the areas wouldn’t otherwise fit in the interval.

A room automation from the blueprint follows the tick of the first area in its Lights & Occupancy. To run your own automation on an area’s tick:

triggers:
  - trigger: event
    event_type: flare_tick
    event_data:
      area_id: kitchen
